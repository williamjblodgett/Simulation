# Expanded Simulation implementation report

Source baseline: `50b0b35473e60a2cdc139cf48a28a998529d0ce3`. Results below are from actual Node/Chromium execution on a shared Windows desktop, not estimates. Each benchmark JSON retains its source fingerprint and every started matrix outcome. No user study was reset, supplied with resources or advanced during browser inspection.

## Scope delivered

The existing worker/runtime, five-agent limit, permanent death, local persistence, scene, navigation and survival planners are retained. New opt-in schema-8 studies have recurring private goals; capability/prerequisite search; measured contextual learning and reusable procedures; real storage, cultivation, treatment and mechanical/electrical component execution; evidence-preserving reports and physical procedure records; and a separate bounded discovery objective. World inspection, Knowledge, Timeline and Run expose the actual records and realized component state. Older studies retain their rules.

This is an **expanded bounded playground**, not completion of arbitrary cave-to-modern invention. Aggregate generators, pumps, cells and relays have authored semantics. Semiconductor fabrication and general-purpose computers are absent. Natural-run results below do not demonstrate an autonomous modern society. The precise implemented limits, supplied priors and quantities are in [OPEN_WORKSHOP.md](OPEN_WORKSHOP.md).

## Predeclared comparisons: 72 simulated hours

All three scenarios are independent **runs**, not nine independent agents: estuary (1 agent, balanced/variable), ridge (3, balanced/harsh), cove (5, scarce/variable). Every arm finished at step 432, remained running, retained all starting agents and passed periodic/final state validation. Continuity and the additional discovery objective were off.

| Decision-directed | 1/1 · 3/3 · 5/5 | 30 | 15 | 0.0796 |
| Development parameters frozen | 1/1 · 3/3 · 5/5 | 26 | 30 | 0.1059 |
| Cost-matched random proposals | 1/1 · 3/3 · 5/5 | 28 | 16 | 0.0699 |

| Fixed protection strategy | 1/1 · 3/3 · 5/5 | 0 | 18 | not applicable |

Interpretation: learning changes expectations and behavior, but survival was tied. Random selection had lower raw prediction error than directed selection in this small matrix. The evidence does **not** establish that the directed policy is superior. Failed actions are factual rejected/failed outcomes, not a proven preventable-death classifier. The fixed arm retains a competent ordinary survival planner and fixes construction strategy, not every action. The frozen arm freezes new development parameters, not the older material learner.

The directed runs made 10/6/9 components, all panels or vessels. They retained 3/8/19 procedures across their agents, including received procedures; these are not independent inventions. Five-agent frozen had six blocked movements including four repeated signatures; directed and random had one blocked movement in the ridge scenario and no repeated signatures across the matrix. Only 0.5/0/1.0 units of water were deposited in directed storage trials, with explicit withdrawal/leakage balances. Empty raw-waste ledgers do not mean unused construction was free: raw stock remains embodied in objects.

Files: `workshop-evaluation-{directed,frozen,random,none,fixed}-survival.json`. These share fingerprint `2557016c0fd9ac884895a10f548a82799b01406660faaf1e9b2bd710f02a5e8f`. ## Traceable prediction and outcome

In `workshop-evaluation-directed-survival.json`, estuary agent `agent-1` created protection goal `development-goal-agent-1-1` from repeated exposure. Its first paid panel test at step 18 (`development-reading-2`) predicted 0.480 and measured 0.157. The same-context prediction at step 33 fell to 0.372 after that evidence. At step 61 it tried a different geometry context, measuring 0.126; the frozen arm retained 0.480 predictions and continued the earlier geometry. These are bounded strategy changes, not a successful diagnosis of the underlying hidden coefficients. The protection target remained unmet/deferred at the end, despite further attempts.

A separate water-access goal (`development-goal-agent-1-4`) paid for a 0.5-unit water trial at step 81 (`development-reading-8`). Predicted retention was 0.550; measured retention was 0.930. The trial did not create water. By run end, 0.442 units had been withdrawn and 0.058 had leaked, matching the 0.5 deposit. That goal was satisfied. Unit tests independently check evidence deduplication, prediction updates, procedure testimony, budget enforcement, urgent interruption and abstention. None of those isolated fixtures is presented as an unaided natural run.

## Performance and bounds

The held-out directed runs took approximately 91 / 121 / 232 seconds of wall time for 72 modeled hours under concurrent desktop load. Step p95 was about 872 / 616 / 2,343 ms, with a 5,697 ms maximum in the five-agent run. Checkpoints peaked near 1.23 / 1.57 / 2.13 MB. These include the whole pre-existing planner and state pipeline, not only new capability search.

The release 168-hour run took 204.7 seconds: p50 30.9 ms, p95 799.7 ms, maximum 3,436.3 ms; largest checkpoint 1.95 MB; final Node heap 94.8 MB and process RSS 384.8 MB. Heap/RSS are snapshots, not peak memory. Different concurrent loads prevent treating these elapsed times as controlled speed comparisons. Worker separation protects rendering, but multi-second planning spikes can still delay updated agent state. No claim of 60 fps, real-phone performance or unbounded storage is made.

Search and memory have explicit caps. The benchmark's contradictory-measurement counter detects residuals over 0.15; it is **not** an automated useful-adaptation classifier. Counted expansions are reported at deliberations, not a profiler of all planner work. More rigorous matched-stream learning tests and prevention/utility classifiers remain future work.

## Remaining limitations and next step

The largest next useful step is to turn a naturally chosen **rotor → shaft or pump → stocked receiver** into a reliably completed, maintained, measured end-to-end strategy under survival constraints, then compare its real resource/travel benefit with moving closer to water or continuing hand collection. Today the executor can run such a system, but natural runs can stop with incomplete arrangements. Simply adding more component names or assigning a build goal would not solve that problem.

Other limits: narrow feature bins, finite budgets, aggregate physics, some physical operations not yet generated by the planner, no autonomous dismantling of connected machinery, no exhaustive long-history provenance proof, no eventual-modernity guarantee, and planning latency spikes. These should guide the next bounded change rather than be hidden by a technology label.
